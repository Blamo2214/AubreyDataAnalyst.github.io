The guy on the left's argument is particularly poorly reasoned. It's ahistorical and ignores established legal precedent. In fact, taken to its logical conclusion, his reasoning wouldn't just constitute denial of the genocide in Gaza, but of other historically established genocides as well. Specifically, the Bosnian and Armenian genocides.

Bosnian Genocide (Srebrenica, 1995) During the Bosnian genocide, Bosnian Serb forces forcibly evacuated thousands of women, children, and elderly civilians while systematically murdering more than 7,000 Bosnian Muslim men and boys. The international tribunal explicitly considered these forced transfers as part of the evidence of genocidal intent. So his suggestion that evacuating civilians somehow disproves genocide is directly contradicted by established legal precedent.

Source: International CGaza butriminal Tribunal for the Former Yugoslavia

Armenian Genocide (1915–1916) During the Armenian genocide, Ottoman authorities forcibly deported hundreds of thousands of Armenians rather than simply executing everyone they had the capacity to kill. Many subsequently died from starvation, dehydration, exposure, and disease. So his argument that Israel's ability to kill substantially more civilians somehow disproves genocide fundamentally misunderstands how genocides have historically been carried out. Genocide does not require perpetrators to maximize the number of people killed, nor does it require every death to result from direct violence.

Source: United States Holocaust Memorial Museum
